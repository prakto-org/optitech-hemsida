## Quick answer

SSPA (Supplier Security and Privacy Assurance) is Microsoft's compliance program for its own suppliers: if your company processes Microsoft personal or confidential data under a contract with Microsoft, you enroll in SSPA and demonstrate compliance with Microsoft's Data Protection Requirements (DPR) annually. Depending on your data processing profile, that means a self-attestation or independent verification. No SSPA status, no Microsoft supplier contract; it's the gate to doing that business.

## How the program works

1. **Enrollment and profiling.** Your company registers in Microsoft's supplier system and answers a data processing profile: what Microsoft data you handle (personal, confidential, or both), where, and how.
2. **The DPR applies by profile.** The Data Protection Requirements catalog covers privacy (notice, purpose limitation, data subject cooperation, subprocessor management, retention and deletion) and security (access control, encryption, incident notification to Microsoft, secure development). Your profile determines which sections bind you.
3. **Annual attestation.** Suppliers self-attest compliance each year; higher-risk profiles must provide independent assurance, typically an assessment by an approved third party, or leverage recognized certifications like [ISO 27001](/faqs/what-is-iso-27001) with appropriate scope to reduce the verification burden.
4. **Status gates contracts.** Procurement checks SSPA status before work; a lapsed or red status blocks new engagements.

## Who this catches

Any vendor in Microsoft's supply chain that touches their data: marketing agencies handling customer lists, consultancies with access to internal information, software and services vendors processing telemetry or support data, and staffing firms embedded in Microsoft projects. The pattern generalizes: like [CJIS for law enforcement](/faqs/what-is-cjis) or [TISAX for automotive](/faqs/what-is-tisax), it's a customer-mandated regime; the customer just happens to be one of the world's largest companies with a formalized program.

## The efficient response

The DPR's substance overlaps your standard privacy and security program: [GDPR machinery](/faqs/what-is-gdpr) covers most privacy requirements, and an ISMS covers most security ones. The work is mapping, evidencing, and keeping the annual cycle from becoming a scramble; see [running SSPA on OptiTech](/faqs/microsoft-sspa-compliance-optitech).
