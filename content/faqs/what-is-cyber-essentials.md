## Quick answer

Cyber Essentials is the UK's government-backed cybersecurity certification: five technical control themes (firewalls, secure configuration, access control, malware protection, and security update management) that block the bulk of commodity attacks. It comes in two levels: Cyber Essentials (a verified self-assessment) and Cyber Essentials Plus (the same controls, independently tested). UK government contracts involving certain data require it, and UK enterprises increasingly expect it from suppliers.

## The five control themes

1. **Firewalls**: boundary and host firewalls configured to block unauthenticated inbound access.
2. **Secure configuration**: default passwords gone, unnecessary services off, device locking enforced.
3. **User access control**: least privilege, unique accounts, MFA where available, and admin accounts separated from daily use.
4. **Malware protection**: anti-malware or application allow-listing on in-scope devices.
5. **Security update management**: supported software only, with high-severity patches applied within 14 days.

The 14-day patch rule is the requirement organizations most often fail: it's concrete, dated, and checked. Scope covers the devices and services that access organizational data, including cloud services and, notably, BYOD used for work.

## When it becomes mandatory for you

- **UK public sector contracts**: required for central government contracts involving handling of certain personal or sensitive data, and common in wider public procurement.
- **Supply chains**: UK primes flow it down to suppliers, similar in mechanism to [CMMC](/faqs/what-is-cmmc) or [NIS 2](/faqs/what-is-nis-2) flow-down.
- **Insurance and due diligence**: some UK cyber insurance offers and enterprise questionnaires treat it as the baseline sanity check.

## Where it sits in a bigger program

Cyber Essentials is deliberately narrow: technical hygiene, annually renewed, cheap and fast compared to [ISO 27001](/faqs/what-is-iso-27001).
